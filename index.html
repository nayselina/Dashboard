Design notes

Part III: what each labeled component does and why.

Primary user: Program Chair. Chairs make the decisions closest to the classroom: assigning teaching loads, opening or merging sections, planning faculty mentoring and referring at-risk classes for review. Everything here is a proposed enhancement. The current portal and SETE do not share data, so the linked views assume the proposed integration by faculty ID, subject code, section and semester.

Labeled components (Overview page)

  1. Header. Shows the app name, the signed-in role and shortcuts to each page.
  2. Filters (interactive feature 1). Academic year and semester, including Summer 1 and Summer 2, update every number and chart together, on every page. College and department are fixed to the chair's assignment.
  3. KPI 1: Average SETE rating. Response-weighted score for the term. Target: 3.50 or higher.
  4. KPI 2: SETE response rate. Shows how far the ratings can be trusted. Target: 50% or higher.
  5. KPI 3: Pass rate. Share of class enrollments that ended in a passing grade. Target: 75% or higher.
  6. KPI 4: Failing grades at midterm. An early warning before final grades. It counts class enrollments, so a student failing two classes counts twice. Target: under 15% of class enrollments.
  7. KPI 5: Grades submitted on time. Share of classes that met the Registrar's deadline. Target: 90% or higher.
  8. Alerts (warning mechanism). Threshold rules flag classes, explain why and link to the full follow-up list. The chair can mark each one as reviewed.
  9. Visualization 1: Scatter plot. The view only integration makes possible. Each dot is a class, and the four zones separate a teaching concern from a student-support concern.
  10. Visualization 2: Small-multiple line charts. One panel per SETE dimension across six regular semesters, against the college average. Summer terms stay out of the lines because each has just one course.
  11. Visualization 3: Horizontal bar chart. Ranks courses by pass rate against the 75% target. Selecting a bar opens that course's classes.
  12. Drill-down (interactive feature 2). Labeled on the scatter plot and bar chart, and available wherever the pointer becomes a hand. KPI cards and bars open the class list. Alerts and dots open a details panel with final grades, previous offerings, dimension scores, response confidence, comment themes and a suggested action.
  13. Confidentiality notice. States the access and privacy rules where users can see them.

Course codes, titles, units, year levels and terms follow the BS IT curriculum on the student portal. Sections, enrollment, grades, SETE ratings, comment themes, faculty profiles and teaching assignments are invented. Students are assumed to move in block sections. Student headcounts are estimated from the largest class in each block, and the student-outcome targets (average grade 2.50, INC under 4%, drops under 3%, 30% midterm recovery, 85% continuation, 80% gateway watch line) are assumptions. All targets, the section capacities (45 students, or 30 for smaller NSTP components), the 18-unit load limit and the split of summer courses into Summer 1 and Summer 2 are assumptions. A live system would use the university's official values.

Guide questions

What information should appear first?

The five KPI cards appear directly under the filters, followed by the alerts. The chair first sees how the term went and which classes need action. Charts come next, for anyone who wants to know why, and the other pages hold the details.

Which visualization best presents SETE results across semesters or dimensions?

Small-multiple line charts. Lines show change over time, and one panel per dimension shows all four without tangled lines. All panels share one scale, so they compare fairly, and the college average gives context.

How can users filter by academic year, semester, college or department?

A single filter row sits above the content on every page and stays in view on larger screens. The chair chooses the academic year and the semester: 1st Semester, 2nd Semester, Summer 1 or Summer 2. College and department are shown but locked, because the account sets them. Every component recalculates from the same selection, so the numbers always agree, and Reset restores the default view. Roles with wider access, such as a dean, would be able to change these two filters.

How does the dashboard identify academic concerns or teaching improvement opportunities?

Alerts catch classes that cross a threshold. The scatter plot separates three situations: both measures low, low SETE only (mentoring or peer observation) and low pass rate only (prerequisites, section size or remedial sessions). The Faculty page suggests mentoring and possible mentors, and the SETE page groups recurring comment themes. Results prompt a review. They do not assign blame, because SETE measures perception and should be read with other evidence.

How does the dashboard protect confidential evaluations and student information?

Role-based access limits the chair to their assigned college and department, so the filters offer no other department to open. Other departments appear only inside the college average. SETE scores with fewer than 5 respondents are hidden, comments appear only as themes, and students are shown as counts. A live system would also need secure sign-in, access logs and compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173). Counseling and health records stay out of the dashboard entirely.
